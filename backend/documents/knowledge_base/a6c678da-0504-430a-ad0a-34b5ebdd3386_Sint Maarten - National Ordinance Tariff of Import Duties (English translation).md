# Sint Maarten (Dutch part) — National Ordinance Tariff of Import Duties (English translation)

> **Unofficial English translation** of the *Landsverordening tarief van invoerrechten* of Sint Maarten, as published at
> <https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR142349> (Dutch, consolidated text). The Dutch text is the only authoritative one. The translation was prepared for the
> Majestic P.L.T. import-tax calculator and was not reviewed by a lawyer or by Sint Maarten Customs.
>
> **Key facts for the calculator**
> - Art. 2(2): the import duty on goods imported into Sint Maarten is **NAf 0** (zero). The duty is therefore the same for every tariff line.
> - The tariff annex (the list of goods and the general rules of interpretation, Art. 1(2)(c)) is **not part of the published text**; it is kept for inspection at the
>   Customs Inspectorate (Art. 5).
> - The Council of Ministers may change the amounts or percentages by decree (Art. 4(2)) and may set temporary exemptions (Art. 4(6)).
> - A flat-rate tariff for travellers' baggage and small consignments for private persons may be set by decree (Art. 2(4)).
> - Customs value follows the WTO-style hierarchy of Chapter II (transaction value first; freight and insurance up to the place of entry are added).
> - The 5% turnover tax (BBO) and the excise on unleaded gasoline are **not in this law**.
>
> Translator's notes: "landsbesluit, houdende algemene maatregelen" (a decree containing general administrative measures, issued by the Council of Ministers) is
> rendered "National Decree containing general measures"; "Landscourant" is the National Gazette; "binnenland" ("the interior") is rendered "Sint Maarten" / "the
> domestic territory"; "NAf" is the Netherlands Antillean guilder (ƒ), the legal currency units used in the text. Text in square brackets is as printed in the source
> ("[vervallen]" = lapsed, repealed). Where the source text has an evident numbering or reference slip, it is translated as printed and flagged with *[sic]*.

## Chapter I — General provisions

### Article 1

1. The provisions of the National Ordinance on Import Duties and Excise ("I.U. en D.") also apply, unless otherwise provided.
2. In this National Ordinance and the provisions based on it, unless otherwise provided, the following mean:
   a. **Minister**: the Minister of Finance;
   b. **customs authorities**: the Inspector of Customs or the officials designated by him;
   c. **tariff of import duties**: the list of goods in the annex to this National Ordinance and the general rules for their interpretation, stating amounts and percentages;
   d. **import duties**: duties based on the tariff of import duties;
   e. **domestic territory** ("binnenland"): Sint Maarten;
   f. **import**: carrying out the formalities required for goods to obtain the status of Sint Maarten goods;
   g. **goods in free circulation**: goods that have the status of Sint Maarten goods;
   h. **exemption**: the import of goods without the levying of import duties;
   i. **person**: a natural person or legal entity established in the domestic territory;
   j. **declarant**: the person who is authorised or obliged to make a declaration.

### Article 2

1. Import duties are due on the import of goods.
2. On the import of goods into Sint Maarten the import duties are **NAf 0,-** (zero).
3. [lapsed]
4. On the import of goods brought in the personal baggage of travellers, and of small consignments intended for private persons, a flat-rate tariff may be levied. The
   flat-rate tariff is set by National Decree containing general measures.

### Article 3

Import duties are levied on the value of the goods, determined on the basis of the provisions of Chapter II, unless otherwise provided.

### Article 4

1. [lapsed]
2. The amounts or percentages contained in the tariff of import duties may be changed by National Decree containing general measures.
3. Within six months after the entry into force of a National Decree containing general measures as referred to in paragraph 2, a draft National Ordinance is submitted to
   the Parliament (Staten) to ratify the provisions of that decree.
4. If the draft National Ordinance referred to in paragraph 3 is not submitted within the prescribed period, if the Parliament does not approve the draft, or if the
   Government withdraws the draft, the National Decree containing general measures is withdrawn by National Decree within one month after, respectively, the expiry of the
   prescribed period, the refusal of approval by the Parliament, or the withdrawal of the draft by the Government.
5. If the draft is enacted as a National Ordinance, the National Decree containing general measures is withdrawn with effect from the moment the National Ordinance enters
   into force.
6. By National Decree containing general measures it may be determined that, for the import of goods designated in it, no import duties are levied during a fixed period,
   or are levied only up to an amount or percentage to be determined in that decree.

### Article 5

1. The annex to the National Ordinance is available for inspection at the Customs Inspectorate.
2. The list of goods referred to in Article 1(2)(c) is accompanied by an explanatory note that is available for inspection at the Customs Inspectorate.
3. Changes to the explanatory note referred to in paragraph 2 apply, as changes to the documents available for inspection, from the moment of their publication.
4. Changes referred to in paragraph 3 are announced in the National Gazette (Landscourant), stating that they have become part of the documents available for inspection.

### Article 6

1. For determining the taxable weight of goods taxed by weight, "net weight" or "weight" without further qualification means: the weight of the good itself, stripped of
   all its packaging.
2. For the application of paragraph 1, the following are regarded as "packaging": all outer and inner packaging means, as well as wrappers, winding means and the like.

### Article 7

1. For the calculation of import duties the value is rounded up to units of one guilder.
2. Parts of a kilogram, a litre or a metre are taken as a whole kilogram, a whole litre or a whole metre respectively; if the quantity on which the import duty must be
   calculated is less than a kilogram, a litre or a metre, parts of those units are taken as a whole hectogram, a whole decilitre or a whole centimetre respectively.
3. The import duties owed by the declarant are, for each consignment of goods declared for import under one item of a declaration, rounded up to the full **NAf 0.10**.

## Chapter II — Customs value

### Title I — General provisions

#### Article 8

1. For the application of this chapter:
   a. **customs value**: the value for the application of the tariff of import duties;
   b. **identical goods**: goods that are the same in all respects, including physical characteristics, quality and reputation, where minor differences in appearance do not
      prevent goods from being regarded as identical;
   c. **similar goods**: goods that, although not alike in all respects, have like characteristics and like component materials which enable them to perform the same
      functions and to be commercially interchangeable.
2. In determining whether goods are similar, factors such as the quality of the goods, their reputation and the presence of a manufacturer's mark or trade mark are taken into
   account.
3. For the application of this chapter, persons are deemed to be related only if:
   a. they are officers or directors of one another's businesses;
   b. they are legally recognised as partners in business;
   c. they are in an employer–employee relationship;
   d. any person directly or indirectly owns, controls or holds 5% or more of the outstanding voting stock or shares of both of them;
   e. one of them directly or indirectly controls the other;
   f. both of them are directly or indirectly controlled by a third person;
   g. together they directly or indirectly control a third person; or
   h. they are members of the same family.
4. A person who acts as sole agent, sole distributor or sole concessionaire, however described, of another is deemed to be related to that other only if one of the criteria in
   paragraph 3 is met.

#### Article 9

Unless otherwise provided, the time for determining the customs value is the time of the declaration for import.

#### Article 10

Anyone directly or indirectly involved in the import of goods must provide the customs authorities, within a period set by them, with all documents and information about that
import that they reasonably consider necessary for the exercise of their powers.

#### Article 11

1. When goods are declared for import whose customs value is determined using the method referred to in Article 17(1)(a), a statement of each separate transaction must be
   submitted.
2. The statement can only be submitted by a declarant who has his residence or place of business in the domestic territory and who has the factual data of the transaction
   concerned.
3. The statement contains all the data necessary to determine the customs value and is drawn up on a form that corresponds to a model established by ministerial regulation.
   The fee at which the forms can be obtained from the Customs Inspectorate is set by National Decree containing general measures.
4. The statement may be omitted if:
   a. the customs value per consignment — not being partial consignments or consignments that are regularly sent by the same sender to the same addressee — does not exceed
      **NAf 7,500**; or
   b. the consignments are incidental ones containing solely goods intended for the personal use of the addressee or members of his family, where the nature and quantities of
      the goods show no commercial purpose.
5. For goods that are regularly imported at the same place and that are the subject of contracts concluded on the same commercial terms between the same seller and the same
   buyer, the customs authorities may, under conditions set by them, allow that the data referred to in paragraph 1 are not all provided with each declaration for import.

#### Article 12

1. The declarant provides a separate statement of the data with which the declared customs value was calculated, if:
   a. a method of valuation is used other than the one referred to in Article 17(1)(a); or
   b. under Article 11(4) a statement as referred to in that article may be omitted, unless the declared customs value is directly apparent from the invoice or other documents.
2. The statement contains at least:
   a. the method of determining the customs value;
   b. a detailed account of how it was calculated; and
   c. where applicable, a reference to a decision taken by the customs authorities under Article 21(4).
3. The declarant submits a copy of the invoice to the customs authorities if the customs value of the goods to be imported is declared on the basis of that invoice. The customs
   authorities keep this copy when the declaration for import was made in writing.

#### Article 13

If it becomes necessary to postpone the final determination of the customs value, the goods can, at the declarant's request, be released against security for the import duties
or other levies that may be owed in the domestic territory.

#### Article 14

1. If the customs authorities deviate from the declaration, they provide, within one month after receipt of a request to that effect, a written explanation of how the customs
   value of the imported goods was determined, except in the cases referred to in Article 18(3).
2. The request referred to in paragraph 1 is submitted in writing to the customs authorities no later than one month after the import duties owed were determined.

#### Article 15

1. When the data for determining the customs value are expressed in a currency other than that of the domestic territory, the exchange rate to be applied is the selling rate,
   last known at the time of import, fixed by the Central Bank of Curaçao and Sint Maarten.
2. By derogation from paragraph 1, it can be determined by ministerial regulation that an exchange rate is used that is fixed weekly by the customs authorities on the basis of
   the average exchange rate of the preceding week.

#### Article 16

When goods have undergone a treatment in an economic zone, or a certain treatment permitted by the customs authorities in a bonded warehouse (entrepôt), then at the declarant's
request, for determining the amount of the import duties, the type, customs value and quantity are used that would have been taken into account had the goods not undergone the
said treatments.

### Title II — Methods of determining the customs value

#### Section 1 — General provisions

##### Article 17

1. The customs value of goods to be imported is determined by applying:
   a. the transaction value method;
   b. the transaction value of identical goods;
   c. the transaction value of similar goods;
   d. the deductive method;
   e. the computed value method; or
   f. the "reasonable means" method (fall-back method).
2. The customs value is determined with the method referred to in paragraph 1(a) when the conditions of Section 2 are met. The method is applied in accordance with that
   section.
3. If a declared customs value cannot be accepted on the basis of the method referred to in paragraph 1(a), it is determined with the method referred to in paragraph 1(b),
   unless the possibility of an adjustment of the customs value is provided for. The method is applied in accordance with Section 3.
4. If the customs value of goods cannot be determined with the methods referred to in paragraph 1(a) and (b), it is determined with the method referred to in paragraph 1(c).
   The method is applied in accordance with Section 4.
5. If the customs value of goods cannot be determined with the methods referred to in paragraph 1(a) to (c), it is determined with the method referred to in paragraph 1(d).
   The method is applied in accordance with Section 5. At the importer's request, however, the method referred to in paragraph 1(e) can be applied in accordance with
   paragraph 6.
6. If the customs value of goods cannot be determined with the methods referred to in paragraph 1(a) to (d), it is determined with the method referred to in paragraph 1(e).
   The method is applied in accordance with Section 6.
7. If the customs value of goods cannot be determined with the methods referred to in paragraph 1(a) to (e), it is determined with the method referred to in paragraph 1(f).
   The method is applied in accordance with Section 7.

##### Article 18

1. If, in the opinion of the customs authorities, insufficient certainty or information has been obtained about the authenticity or accuracy of the statements, documents or
   declarations submitted for determining the customs value, they are authorised not to accept the declared customs value.
2. The customs authorities need not determine the customs value of imported goods with the transaction value method if there is reasonable doubt whether the declared value
   corresponds to the price actually paid or payable as described in Article 19.
3. When the doubt referred to in paragraph 2 remains with the customs authorities, they inform the person concerned, in writing if requested, of the reasons for that doubt and
   give him a reasonable opportunity to respond.
4. The determination of the customs value is communicated to the person concerned by a reasoned decision.

#### Section 2 — The transaction value

##### Article 19

1. The customs value of the goods to be imported is the transaction value, being the price actually paid or payable for the goods when sold for export to the domestic
   territory, adjusted in accordance with Articles 21 and 22.
2. The transaction value is accepted as the customs value unless:
   a. there are restrictions on the disposal or use of the goods by the buyer, other than restrictions that:
      1°. are imposed or required by law or the authorities in the domestic territory;
      2°. limit the geographical area in which the goods may be resold; or
      3°. do not substantially affect the value of the goods;
   b. the sale or price is subject to some condition or consideration for which a value cannot be determined;
   c. part of the proceeds of any subsequent resale, disposal or use of the goods by the buyer will accrue directly or indirectly to the seller, and an adjustment in accordance
      with Article 21 is not possible;
   d. the buyer and the seller are related and the transaction value is not acceptable under Article 20.
3. The price actually paid or payable is the total payment made or to be made by the buyer to or for the benefit of the seller for the imported goods.
4. The payment may be made by transfer of money and by means of letters of credit or negotiable instruments. The payment may be made directly or indirectly.
5. Activities undertaken by the buyer, other than those for which an adjustment is provided in Article 21, are not regarded as an indirect payment to the seller and are
   regarded as undertaken on the buyer's own account, even if the buyer is obliged to do so under an agreement with the seller.
6. The activities referred to in paragraph 5 include activities related to the marketing of the goods, such as market research, advertising and sales promotion of the goods
   concerned, as well as activities carried out by the buyer on his own account in connection with the warranty given for the goods.
7. When goods declared for import form part of a larger quantity of the same goods purchased in a single transaction, the customs value is calculated by a proportional
   apportionment of the price actually paid or payable, in the same proportion as the declared quantity bears to the total quantity purchased.
8. A proportional apportionment of the price actually paid or payable is also applied in the case of partial loss or damage before the declaration for import of the goods
   whose customs value is being determined.
9. If the goods have been used in a third country between the time of the sale and the time of the declaration for import, application of the transaction value is not
   mandatory.

##### Article 20

1. In a sale between related persons the transaction value is accepted if:
   a. the relationship has not influenced the price; or
   b. at the same or about the same time that value closely approximates one of the following values:
      1°. the customs value of identical or similar goods in sales for export to the domestic territory between buyers and sellers who are not related;
      2°. the customs value of identical or similar goods as determined with the deductive method.
2. In determining whether the transaction value is acceptable, the fact that the buyer and the seller are related is not in itself sufficient ground to regard the
   transaction value as unacceptable.
3. In applying the criteria in paragraph 1(b), account is taken of differences in commercial level, quantities, the elements referred to in Articles 21 and 22, and of costs
   borne by the seller in sales to unrelated buyers that he does not bear in sales to buyers who are related to him.
4. When the customs authorities, on the basis of information from the declarant or information otherwise obtained, have reason to assume that the relationship has influenced
   the price, they communicate those reasons to the declarant, who is given a reasonable period to respond. If the declarant so requests, he is informed of those reasons in
   writing.
5. The declarant must demonstrate that the criterion in paragraph 1(b) is met.

##### Article 21

1. In determining the customs value by applying Article 19, the price actually paid or payable for the goods to be imported is increased solely by:
   a. the following elements, to the extent that they are borne by the buyer and are not included in the price actually paid or payable for the goods:
      1°. commissions and brokerage, except buying commissions as referred to in Article 22(f);
      2°. the cost of packaging means that are, for customs purposes, deemed to form one whole with the goods, it being understood that the cost of packaging means that will
          be used for more than one import is, at the declarant's request, apportioned proportionally;
      3°. the cost of packing, both labour and materials;
   b. the proportionally apportioned value of the following goods and services where supplied directly or indirectly by the buyer free of charge or at a reduced price for use
      in connection with the production and sale for export of the imported goods, to the extent that this value is not included in the price actually paid or payable:
      1°. materials, components, parts and similar goods incorporated in the imported goods;
      2°. tools, dies, moulds and similar items used in the production of the imported goods;
      3°. materials consumed in the production of the imported goods;
      4°. engineering, development, artwork, design work, plans and sketches undertaken or made abroad and necessary for the production of the imported goods, not including
          the cost of scientific research and preliminary design sketches;
   c. royalties and licence fees, not including payments for the right to reproduce the imported goods in the domestic territory, that relate to the goods being valued and
      that the buyer must pay, directly or indirectly, as a condition of the sale, to the extent that those royalties and licence fees are not included in the price actually paid
      or payable;
   d. the value of that part of the proceeds of any subsequent resale, disposal or use of the imported goods that accrues directly or indirectly to the seller; *[sic: in the
      published text the following two items are printed under (d), numbered 1° and 2°]*
      1°. the cost of transport and insurance of the imported goods; and
      2°. the cost of loading and handling of the imported goods in connection with transport to the place of entry of the goods into the domestic territory.
2. If the transport referred to in paragraph 1(d) takes place free of charge or with a means of transport of the buyer and the goods are not insured for transport, the freight
   and insurance costs to the place of entry, calculated according to the usual rates for transport and insurance with the same kind of means of transport, must be included in
   the customs value.
3. When elements are added to the price actually paid or payable by application of this article, this is done solely on the basis of objective and quantifiable data.
4. If the cost of an element relates to several transactions, or other factors must be taken into account in the calculation, the customs authorities decide on the addition of
   that element per declaration for import.

##### Article 22

The following costs, payments and commissions do not form part of the customs value determined in accordance with Article 19, provided they are distinguished from the price
actually paid or payable:

a. costs of construction, installation, assembly, maintenance or technical assistance, undertaken after the import and relating to the imported goods;
b. import duties and other taxes owed in the domestic territory on the import or sale of the goods;
c. costs of storage and of keeping the goods in good condition during storage in customs warehousing facilities, bonded warehouses or economic zones established in the
   domestic territory, when the customs value is based on a price actually paid or payable in which these costs are included;
d. an amount of internal taxes to which the goods concerned are subject in the country of origin or export, provided it can be shown to the satisfaction of the customs
   authorities that those goods were or will be exempted from them for the benefit of the buyer;
e. payments for the right to reproduce the imported goods in the domestic territory;
f. buying commissions, being the fees that an importer pays to his agent for the service of representing him in the purchase of the goods being valued;
g. costs of transport and insurance, and of handling of the imported goods in connection with transport, after entry into the domestic territory, except postal charges of goods
   sent by post;
h. interest under a financing agreement entered into by the buyer in connection with the purchase of imported goods, does not form part of the customs value if:
   i. the interest is distinguished from the price actually paid or payable for the goods;
   ii. the financing agreement was made in writing;
   iii. such goods are sold at the price declared as the price actually paid or payable; and
   iv. the interest rate charged does not exceed the level customary for such transactions in the country where, and at the time when, the financing took place; *[sic: the
       published text is cut off here; the item that follows is printed as "i."]*
i. a cash-payment discount of up to a maximum of 2% of the invoice value.

#### Section 3 — The transaction value of identical goods

##### Article 23

1. The customs value of goods determined by applying this article is based on the transaction value of identical goods sold for export to the domestic territory and exported
   at the same or approximately the same time as the goods being valued.
2. To determine the transaction value referred to in paragraph 1, the transaction value of identical goods sold at the same commercial level and in the same or approximately
   the same quantity as the goods being valued is used. In the absence of such a sale, the transaction value of identical goods sold at a different commercial level or in
   different quantities is used, adjusted to take account of the differences in commercial level or quantity, provided such adjustments can be based on evidence clearly
   showing that they are reasonable and accurate, whether they lead to an increase or a decrease in value.
3. If the costs referred to in Article 21(1)(d) are included in the transaction value, that value is adjusted to take account of significant differences that may exist,
   owing to different distances and modes of transport, between the costs for the imported goods and for the identical goods considered.
4. If, in applying this article, more than one transaction value of identical goods is found, the lowest of those values is used to determine the customs value of the imported
   goods.

#### Section 4 — The transaction value of similar goods

##### Article 24

1. The customs value of goods determined by applying this article is based on the transaction value of similar goods sold for export to the domestic territory and exported at
   the same or approximately the same time as the goods being valued.
2. Article 23(2) to (4) apply correspondingly to determining the customs value using the transaction value of similar goods.

#### Section 5 — The deductive method

##### Article 25

1. The customs value of goods determined by applying this article is based on the price at which the goods to be imported, or imported identical or similar goods, are resold,
   in the condition in which they were imported, at or about the time of import.
2. To determine the price referred to in paragraph 1, the unit price is used at which those goods are resold in the greatest aggregate quantity at the first commercial level
   after import at which such sales take place, to persons who are not related to the seller.
3. If such sales do not take place at or about the time of import, the resale price on the earliest date after import is used, but within 90 days after that import.
4. From the price the following elements are deducted, to the extent that they are included in it:
   a. the commission usually paid or agreed, or the usual mark-up for profit and general expenses, on sales in the domestic territory of goods of the same nature or kind;
   b. the usual costs of transport and insurance and associated costs incurred in the domestic territory; and
   c. the import duties and other taxes owed in the domestic territory on the import or sale of the goods.
5. If neither the goods to be imported nor imported identical or similar goods are sold in the domestic territory in the condition in which they were imported, then at the
   declarant's request the customs value is based on the price at which the imported goods, after further processing, are resold to persons in the domestic territory who are not
   related to the persons from whom they buy the goods, less the value added by the processing and the deductions referred to in paragraph 4.

#### Section 6 — The computed value method

##### Article 26

The customs value of imported goods, determined by applying this article, is based on a computed value, being the sum of:

a. the cost or value of the materials and of manufacture or other processing employed in producing the imported goods;
b. an amount for profit and general expenses equal to that usually reflected in sales by producers in the country of export of goods of the same nature or kind as the goods
   being valued, destined for export to the domestic territory;
c. 1°. the cost of transport and insurance of the imported goods; and
   2°. the cost of loading and handling of the imported goods in connection with transport to the place of entry of the goods into the domestic territory.

#### Section 7 — The "reasonable means" method

##### Article 27

1. The determination of the customs value with the "reasonable means" method is made in accordance with the principles of the valuation methods referred to in Articles 19
   and 23 and is based as far as possible on data available in the domestic territory and, where possible, on previously determined customs values of identical or similar goods.
2. The customs value determined by applying this article is not based on:
   a. the selling price in the domestic territory of goods produced in the domestic territory;
   b. the price of goods on the domestic market of the country of export;
   c. the price of goods for export from the country of origin;
   d. minimum customs values;
   e. arbitrary or fictitious values.

## Chapter III — Exemptions

### Title I — Definitions

#### Article 28

For the application of this chapter:

a. **import duties**: import duties, as well as special import duties owed on import;
b. **permit holder**: a person to whom a permit has been granted under this National Ordinance;
c. **exemption document**: the document issued on a declaration for import with exemption from import duties;
d. **personal goods**: goods intended for the personal use of the person concerned at the import, or of the members of his family;
e. **alcoholic products**: alcoholic beverages intended for human consumption;
f. **means of transport**:
   1°. passenger vehicles;
   2°. means for the commercial transport of persons and goods;
   3°. pleasure craft;
g. **passenger vehicles**: motor vehicles, other than sports vehicles or vehicles for pursuing a hobby, on two or more wheels, including trailers for purposes other than the
   commercial transport of persons or goods, and suitable for use on the public road;
h. **pleasure craft**: vessels other than those which, by their type of construction and equipment, are suitable and intended for the commercial transport of persons or goods.

#### Article 29

1. For the application of this chapter, *normal place of residence* means the place where a natural person lives for at least 183 days per calendar year because of personal
   and occupational ties or, if he has no occupational ties, because of personal ties showing close links between himself and the place where he lives.
2. If the person concerned has his occupational ties in a place other than his personal ties and as a result lives alternately in different countries, he is deemed to have his
   normal place of residence in the country of his personal ties, provided he returns there at regular intervals. If the person concerned is at the place of his occupational
   ties for an assignment of a fixed duration, he is nevertheless deemed to have his normal place of residence in the country of his personal ties.
3. The fact that education is received abroad does not in itself mean that the normal place of residence has moved abroad.
4. The normal place of residence must be demonstrated. If the customs authorities so request, additional information and evidence are submitted.

### Title II — General provisions

#### Article 30

1. Unless expressly provided otherwise, a permit is required for import with exemption. A permit may be one-off or continuing. Rules or restrictions may be attached to a permit.
2. The permit must be obtained before the declaration for import or, where applicable, before the export of the goods preceding the import. In appropriate cases a permit may
   be granted on the declaration.
3. The person concerned submits a timely written application to the customs authorities for that purpose. He attaches to the application all data and documents and provides all
   information needed for a proper assessment of the application. When the customs authorities consider it necessary, they may set a period within which the applicant must
   submit additional evidence.
4. The customs authorities decide within **nine months** of receipt of the application. The decision is made in writing and is reasoned.
5. The permit holder must notify the customs authorities of any fact occurring after the permit is issued that may affect the maintenance or content of the permit. If asked,
   the permit holder must point out to the customs authorities the goods to which the exemption relates.
6. At the request of the person concerned, the refusal of an exemption for which no permit is required is put in writing.

#### Article 31

1. If the exemption depends on the condition that the goods are re-exported, or were exported beforehand, or follow a certain destination, the identity of the goods must, in the
   opinion of the customs authorities, be ensured.
2. In the cases referred to in paragraph 1 the customs authorities may require that a goods account is kept and, unless expressly provided otherwise, that security is
   provided.
3. If the identity of the goods cannot be ensured, the customs authorities may decide not to apply the exemption.

#### Article 32

1. For goods imported with exemption on condition that the goods are re-exported or follow a certain destination, the customs authorities may, at the permit holder's request,
   give permission:
   a. to waive the exemption; or
   b. to destroy the goods under official supervision.
2. Article 30(3) applies correspondingly.
3. Permission is granted only if special circumstances which could not be foreseen at the import prevent the goods from being re-exported or from being given the destination
   for which the exemption was granted.
4. Permission as referred to in paragraph 1(a) may also be granted when the goods are surrendered free of all costs to the Country (Land). In that case the permit holder is
   exempt from paying import duties.
5. For goods for which the exemption is waived, a declaration for import is made at the same time as the request. Unless otherwise provided, the import duties owed are
   determined according to the tariff of import duties at the time the goods were imported with exemption.
6. A declaration is made for the import of remains and waste of goods that have been destroyed under official supervision.
7. The person concerned reports without delay the destruction of goods which, in his opinion, is due to accidental circumstances or force majeure. The customs authorities may
   treat this destruction of goods as destruction under official supervision. Paragraph 6 applies to the remains and waste.

#### Article 33

1. The customs authorities may withdraw the permit for import with exemption if:
   a. the conditions and provisions laid down by or pursuant to this chapter are not, or not timely, or not properly complied with;
   b. the permit was issued on the basis of incorrect or incomplete data provided by the applicant and the permit would not have been granted had the correct data been known;
   c. the permit holder is granted a suspension of payments or is declared bankrupt.
2. The withdrawal of the permit is reasoned.

#### Article 34

It is prohibited:

a. not to comply, or not to comply in time or properly, with the conditions and provisions laid down by or pursuant to this chapter;
b. to provide incorrect or incomplete data the result of which is or could be that exemption is enjoyed without entitlement to it;
c. to give or have given to the goods a destination other than the one for which the permit for import with exemption was granted.

### Title III — Exemptions from import duties

#### Section 1 — Exemptions on definitive import

##### § 1 Removal goods (household effects on moving)

###### Article 35

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of personal goods by natural persons who transfer their normal place of residence to the domestic territory.
2. The exemption is limited to personal goods that:
   a. have been in the possession of the person concerned for at least six months before the date on which he gave up his normal place of residence in the country of origin
      and, in the case of non-consumable goods, have been used by him at his former normal place of residence; and
   b. are intended to be used by the person concerned for the same purpose at his new normal place of residence.
3. At the request of the customs authorities the declarant demonstrates that paragraph 2(a) is met.
4. The exemption is granted only if:
   a. a list signed by the person concerned is submitted, containing a description of all goods for which exemption is claimed;
   b. the goods are declared for import before the expiry of a period of six months, counted from the date on which the person concerned established his normal place of
      residence in the domestic territory.
5. By derogation from paragraph 2(a), exemption is granted for the import of passenger vehicles that form part of the personal goods of the person concerned if the passenger
   vehicle was demonstrably and continuously in the possession and use of the person concerned for at least one year before the date of embarkation in the country of origin,
   whereby:
   a. the exemption is limited to one passenger vehicle per family or household; and
   b. in cases giving cause, the customs authorities may have the person concerned provide security.
6. Alcoholic products and tobacco and tobacco products, to the extent they cannot be regarded as normal household stock, as well as means of transport other than passenger
   vehicles, are excluded from the exemption.

###### Article 36

1. The import of personal goods may take place in instalments, provided it is within the period referred to in Article 35(4)(b).
2. In special cases the customs authorities may allow derogation from the periods referred to in Article 35(4)(b) and in the opening words of Article 35(5).

###### Article 37

The customs authorities may provide in the permit that in the declarations for import made for the goods the kind of goods may be stated as: "household effects" (verhuisgoed).

###### Article 38

Within 12 months, counted from the date on which the declaration for import was accepted, the personal goods imported with exemption may not be lent, pledged, hired out or
transferred, whether for consideration or free of charge, without the prior permission of the customs authorities.

##### § 2 Inherited goods

###### Article 39

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of personal goods that:
   a. have been acquired by inheritance by a natural person who has his normal place of residence in the domestic territory; or
   b. have been acquired by testamentary inheritance by a legal entity that carries on a non-profit activity and is established in the domestic territory.
2. Alcoholic products, tobacco and tobacco products and commercial vehicles are excluded from the exemption.
3. The exemption is granted only if:
   a. a statement issued by a notary or comparable official of the country of export is submitted, showing that the goods were acquired by inheritance;
   b. a list signed by the person concerned is submitted, containing a description of all goods for which exemption is claimed; and
   c. the goods are declared for import within one year of the date on which they came into the possession of the person concerned.

###### Article 40

1. The import of the personal goods may take place in instalments within the period referred to in Article 39(3)(c).
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances.

###### Article 41

1. The customs authorities may provide in the permit that in the declarations for import made for the goods the kind of goods may be stated as: "inherited goods" (erfgoed).
2. Paragraph 1 does not apply to motor vehicles.

##### § 3 Students' movable property

###### Article 42

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of personal goods by:
   a. school pupils and students who come to live in the domestic territory for the purposes of their studies;
   b. those who come to live in the domestic territory after completing their studies abroad.
2. A pupil or student within the meaning of paragraph 1 is any person who is enrolled as a regular student at a university or at an educational institution for full-time
   higher or secondary vocational education.
3. The exemption is limited to:
   a. used movable property which forms the normal furnishing of a student room;
   b. linen and clothing, both used and new; and
   c. study materials, being the objects and instruments normally used by a pupil or student in his studies.
4. The exemption is granted only if a list signed by the person concerned is submitted, containing a description of all goods for which exemption is claimed.
5. Article 38 applies correspondingly.

###### Article 43

The customs authorities may provide in the permit that in the declarations for import made for the goods the kind of goods may be stated as: "students' movable property"
(roerende goederen studenten).

##### § 4 Consignments of negligible value

###### Article 44

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for consignments sent by letter post or as a postal parcel directly to a person in the domestic territory and containing goods of a
   total value not exceeding **NAf 50**.
2. Alcoholic products and tobacco and tobacco products are excluded from the exemption.

###### Article 45

No permit is required for import with this exemption.

##### § 5 Consignments from private persons to private persons

###### Article 46

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods contained in a consignment sent by a private person to another private person who is in the domestic
   territory, if the consignment:
   a. is of an occasional nature;
   b. contains solely personal goods;
   c. is sent by the sender to the addressee without the latter making any payment for it; and
   d. has a value of no more than **NAf 125**, including the value of the goods referred to in paragraph 2.
2. For alcoholic products, tobacco and tobacco products, perfumery, toilet waters, coffee and tea, and essences and extracts of these, the exemption is limited to quantities
   to be set by ministerial regulation for each of these goods.

###### Article 47

No permit is required for import with this exemption.

##### § 6 Personal baggage of travellers

###### Article 48

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods forming part of the personal baggage of travellers, insofar as it concerns:
   a. baggage that the traveller:
      1°. can declare to Customs on his arrival in the domestic territory; or
      2°. declares to Customs later, and which on his departure from abroad was registered as accompanied baggage with the carrier that transported him to the domestic
          territory; and
   b. imports that are of an occasional nature and relate solely to personal goods.
2. For alcoholic products, tobacco and tobacco products, perfumery, toilet waters, coffee and tea, and essences and extracts of these, the exemption is limited to quantities
   per traveller to be set by ministerial regulation for each of these goods.
3. The exemption is not granted for alcoholic products and tobacco and tobacco products imported by travellers under 17 years of age.
4. For goods that show no signs of use or that are of a special or valuable nature, no exemption is granted unless it is shown to the satisfaction of the customs authorities
   that they were previously exported from free circulation.

###### Article 49

1. For goods acquired abroad, the exemption is granted only up to a total value of **NAf 200**.
2. For travellers under 15 years of age the amount referred to in paragraph 1 is limited to **NAf 65**.
3. If the total value of the goods referred to in paragraph 1 exceeds the maximum applicable under paragraph 1 or 2, the exemption applies only to goods with a value up to those
   amounts, it being understood that the value of an individual item may not be split.

###### Article 50

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally.

##### § 7 Objects of an educational, scientific or cultural nature

###### Article 51

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of publications and documents of an educational, scientific or cultural nature, provided the nature and quantity of the
goods show no commercial purpose.

###### Article 52

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of visual and auditory material of an educational, scientific or cultural nature designated by ministerial regulation.
2. The exemption is granted only if the material was obtained by donation from a natural person or legal entity established abroad and is intended for:
   a. public institutions and public-utility organisations of an educational, scientific or cultural nature designated by ministerial regulation; or
   b. private institutions and organisations that work mainly in the field of education, science or culture, designated by ministerial regulation.

###### Article 53

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of objects that form part of collections and works of art of an educational, scientific or cultural nature, insofar as
these goods:

a. are not intended for sale; and
b. are intended for the following, designated by ministerial regulation:
   1°. public institutions and organisations, as well as public-utility institutions and organisations, of an educational, scientific or cultural nature; or
   2°. museums, art galleries or other institutions.

##### § 8 Articles for persons with disabilities

###### Article 54

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. objects specially designed for the educational, scientific and cultural development of persons with disabilities;
   b. objects specially designed to support the employment and improve the social position of persons with disabilities;
   c. spare parts, components and specific accessories intended for the objects referred to in (a) and (b).
2. The exemption is granted only if the objects are imported by:
   a. the persons with disabilities themselves for their own use; or
   b. institutions and organisations, to be designated by the Minister, that are mainly engaged in the educational, scientific and cultural development of persons with
      disabilities, or in supporting the employment and improving the social position of persons with disabilities.
3. For the application of paragraph 2(a), the customs authorities may require that the disability is demonstrated by a medical statement.

###### Article 55

1. Goods imported with exemption may not be lent, pledged, hired out or transferred, whether for consideration or free of charge, without the prior permission of the customs
   authorities.
2. If an object is lent, pledged, hired out or transferred to a person, institution or organisation that qualifies for exemption under Article 52, and that:
   a. uses the object for purposes that entitle to this exemption, the exemption remains in force;
   b. does not use the object for purposes that entitle to this exemption, Article 32 applies, it being understood that the amount of the import duties owed is determined
      according to the tariff of import duties at the time the object is declared for import.
3. If the conditions for qualifying for the exemption are no longer met, or the goods imported with exemption are used for other purposes, paragraph 1 and paragraph 2(b)
   apply correspondingly.

##### § 9 Test sera for blood-group testing

###### Article 56

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. sera of human, animal, plant or other origin, intended for blood-group testing and for detecting blood-group incompatibility;
   b. the special packaging necessary for the transport of the sera;
   c. the solvents and accessories needed for the use of the sera and added to the consignment.
2. The exemption is limited to sera intended for institutions or laboratories designated by ministerial regulation, for use for medical purposes, on condition that no profit is
   made on them.

###### Article 57

No permit is required for import with this exemption.

##### § 10 Pharmaceutical products used on the occasion of international sporting events

###### Article 58

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of pharmaceutical products for medicine intended for use by persons and animals that come from abroad to take part in
international sporting events organised in the domestic territory, insofar as within the limits of their needs during their stay in the domestic territory.

###### Article 59

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally.

##### § 11 Goods for combating disasters

###### Article 60

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for goods imported by the government and other institutions designated by ministerial regulation, in order to be:
   1°. provided free of charge to victims of disasters occurring in the domestic territory; or
   2°. placed at the disposal of the victims of such disasters, but remaining the property of the institutions concerned.
2. The exemption is not granted for goods intended for the reconstruction of the disaster area, unless placed at disposal free of charge by assisting countries or
   international organisations.
3. Only institutions that do not seek profit and that work mainly in the field of relief or charity qualify for designation by ministerial regulation. Designation takes
   place only if the institution's accounts enable the customs authorities to check its operations.
4. No security need be provided for the issue of the exemption document.

###### Article 61

1. Goods that can no longer be used for the purposes for which the exemption was granted may be transferred to another institution that qualifies for designation under
   Article 60, if the customs authorities have given permission and the transfer is free of charge.
2. If a transfer as referred to in paragraph 1 is not free of charge, Article 32 applies, it being understood that the amount of the import duties owed is determined according
   to the tariff of import duties at the time the goods are declared for import.
3. If an institution no longer meets the conditions for qualifying for exemption, or wishes to use the goods imported with exemption for other purposes, paragraph 2 applies
   correspondingly.

###### Article 62

The customs authorities may provide in the permit that in the declarations for import made for the goods the kind of goods may be stated as: "goods for combating disasters".

##### § 12 Honours and awards

###### Article 63

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. decorations awarded by foreign governments to persons who have their normal place of residence in the domestic territory;
   b. cups, medals and similar objects of a mainly symbolic nature, awarded abroad to persons who have their normal place of residence in the domestic territory, as a tribute
      for the activities they have undertaken in fields such as art, science, sport and public service, or as a token of appreciation of their merits on a special occasion, and
      imported by those persons themselves into the domestic territory;
   c. cups, medals and similar objects of a mainly symbolic nature, offered free of charge by authorities or persons established abroad to be awarded in the domestic territory
      for the same purposes as referred to in (b).
2. "Similar objects" as referred to in paragraph 1 means objects with the same function as cups and medals, such as vases, ornamental plates, small sculptures, paintings and
   miniatures.
3. The exemption is granted only insofar as the nature and quantities of the goods show no commercial purpose.

###### Article 64

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import of the goods referred to in Article 63(1)(a) and (b) may be made orally.

##### § 13 Gifts received in the framework of foreign relations

###### Article 65

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of objects:
   a. imported by persons who have made an official visit abroad and who received these objects on that occasion as gifts from the authorities who received them;
   b. imported by persons who come to make an official visit in the domestic territory in order to give these objects on that occasion as gifts to the authorities who receive
      them;
   c. addressed, by way of gift or as a token of friendship or tribute, by an official authority, public body or organisation with legal personality carrying out activities of
      public interest established abroad, to a similar authority, body or organisation in the domestic territory.
2. The exemption is granted only if the objects are intended as an occasional gift and insofar as the nature and quantities of the goods show no commercial purpose.
3. Alcoholic products and tobacco and tobacco products are excluded from the exemption.

###### Article 66

1. No permit is required for import with exemption of the goods referred to in Article 65(1)(a) and (b).
2. The declaration for import of the goods referred to in Article 65(1)(a) and (b) may be made orally.

##### § 14 Goods intended for sovereigns and heads of state

###### Article 67

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:

a. gifts to be presented to reigning sovereigns and heads of state;
b. goods intended to be used or consumed by reigning foreign sovereigns and foreign heads of state, and by the persons officially representing them, during their official stay
   in the domestic territory.

###### Article 68

1. If the goods are imported simultaneously with the official visit, no permit is required for import with exemption.
2. If the goods are imported simultaneously with the official visit, the declaration for import may be made orally.

##### § 15 Samples of goods

###### Article 69

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of samples of goods whose value is negligible and which can serve only to obtain orders for goods of the kind they
   represent, with a view to their import into the domestic territory.
2. "Samples of goods" as referred to in paragraph 1 means articles that are representative of a category of merchandise and that, by reason of their nature, quantity,
   packaging, presentation or markings on them, are unsuitable for use other than for soliciting customers.
3. To assess whether samples are of negligible value, the combined value of all samples forming part of one consignment is taken into account. The values of consignments sent
   by the same sender to different addressees are not added together, even if the consignments are imported simultaneously.
4. The customs authorities may require that certain articles qualify for exemption only if they are rendered permanently unusable by cutting, piercing, affixing a clearly
   visible and indelible mark, or any other process, without this treatment however causing them to lose their character as samples.

##### § 16 Exhibition goods

###### Article 70

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. small samples representative of goods manufactured abroad and intended for exhibitions;
   b. goods imported solely to be demonstrated, or to demonstrate machines and apparatus manufactured abroad, during exhibitions;
   c. materials of low value, such as paint, varnish and wallpaper, used for the construction, fitting out and decoration of temporary stands occupied by foreign exhibitors at
      exhibitions and which are lost through their use as such;
   d. printed matter, catalogues, prospectuses, price lists, posters, calendars (illustrated or not), unframed photographs and other objects supplied free of charge to be used
      for advertising goods manufactured abroad that are displayed at exhibitions.
2. In this section "exhibitions" means:
   a. exhibitions, trade fairs, fairs and similar events in the field of trade, industry, agriculture and crafts;
   b. exhibitions or events organised mainly for charitable purposes;
   c. exhibitions or events organised mainly for a scientific, technical, craft, artistic, educational or cultural, tourist, sporting, religious or ecclesiastical purpose, or to
      promote friendship between peoples;
   d. meetings of representatives of international organisations or groupings;
   e. ceremonies and events of an official or commemorative character.
3. The exemption is not granted if the goods are intended for private exhibitions organised in shops or business premises with a view to the sale of foreign goods.
4. Alcoholic products, tobacco and tobacco products and fuels are excluded from the exemption referred to in paragraph 1(a) and (b).

###### Article 71

1. The exemption referred to in Article 70(1)(a) is granted only if:
   a. the samples are imported as such or are made only during the exhibition from goods imported from abroad in larger quantities;
   b. the samples serve solely to be handed out free of charge to visitors during the exhibition for their personal use or consumption;
   c. the samples can be recognised as advertising material of low value per unit;
   d. the samples are unsuitable for commercial purposes and, where applicable, are offered in packaging containing a smaller quantity than the smallest quantity of the same
      goods obtainable on the market;
   e. samples of foodstuffs and beverages that are not handed out in packaging as referred to in (d) are consumed on the spot at the exhibition;
   f. the total value and quantity of the samples stand in a reasonable proportion to the nature of the exhibition, the number of visitors and the extent of the exhibitor's
      participation.
2. The exemption referred to in Article 70(1)(b) is limited to goods that are consumed or destroyed during the exhibition and whose total value and quantity stand in a
   reasonable proportion to the nature of the exhibition, the number of visitors and the importance of the exhibitor's participation.
3. The exemption referred to in Article 70(1)(d) applies only to printed matter and advertising objects intended solely to be handed out free of charge to the public at the
   place of the exhibition, and whose total value and quantity stand in a reasonable proportion to the nature of the exhibition, the number of visitors and the importance of the
   exhibitor's participation.

##### § 17 Consignments for the Intellectual Property Office

###### Article 72

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of trade marks, models or designs and the related filing dossiers, as well as dossiers concerning applications for patents
and the like, intended for the Intellectual Property Office.

###### Article 73

No permit is required for import with this exemption.

##### § 18 Tourist publicity material

###### Article 74

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of tourist publicity material, insofar as it concerns:

a. documents intended to be handed out free of charge and whose main purpose is to induce the public to visit foreign countries, in particular to attend meetings or events of
   a cultural, tourist, sporting or religious nature or related to a profession, provided these documents contain no more than 25% private commercial advertising and the aim of
   general promotion is clearly apparent from them;
b. lists and yearbooks of foreign hotels, published by official tourist organisations or under their auspices, as well as timetables of transport services operated abroad, when
   these documents are intended to be handed out free of charge and contain no more than 25% private commercial advertising;
c. technical material sent to representatives or correspondents recognised or appointed by official national tourist organisations, and not intended to be handed out.

##### § 19 Miscellaneous goods

###### Article 75

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. 1°. machines, machine parts, tools, implements, implement parts, ironwork, chemicals, fencing wire;
      2°. working horses, pure-bred breeding animals and products of the plant and animal kingdoms;
      3°. prepared animal feed and products of plant or animal origin, mixed or not; and
      4°. minerals, vitamins and chemical compounds evidently intended for the preparation of prepared animal feed,
      provided they are intended to be used solely in the commercial practice of agriculture, livestock farming, horticulture or fishing;
   b. motor vehicles for the commercial transport of goods and tractors, and fishing vessels, that are intended and equipped to be used solely in the commercial practice of
      agriculture, livestock farming, horticulture or fishing;
   c. motor vehicles for the commercial transport of persons which, according to a permit or dispensation issued to the person concerned by the competent authority, are
      intended solely to provide regular public transport or taxi transport;
   d. ships regularly used in commercial traffic with foreign countries, as well as their inventory insofar as it is not unloaded;
   e. [lapsed]
   f. goods to be designated by ministerial regulation, intended to be used in and for the benefit of aviation;
   g. goods exempted under the National Ordinance for the Promotion of Business Establishment and Hotel Construction, the National Ordinance on Tax Facilities for Industrial
      Enterprises, the National Ordinance for the Promotion of Land Development, the National Ordinance on Hotel Renovation, or the National Ordinance on Economic Zones;
   h. [lapsed]
   i. war material for the defence of Sint Maarten, brought in by the Dutch armed forces;
   j. utility goods imported by the Dutch armed forces for the account of the Netherlands for use at the bases;
   k. fish intended for human consumption, live, fresh, chilled or frozen, if in the domestic territory there is no distribution link between the catch and the landing and the
      fish is landed by small fishing vessels;
   l. goods intended for projects that are for the account of the Netherlands or Aruba within the framework of mutual assistance, as well as goods that are for the account of
      international organisations within the framework of development aid, subject to conditions to be set by ministerial regulation;
   m. sound carriers with recordings of performing musicians established in the domestic territory;
   n. materials and supplies which, by their nature and destination, are used solely in hospitals;
   o. used commercial and office documents such as travel tickets, bills of lading and waybills;
   p. press photographs, insofar as this is evident from notes appearing on or attached to them, sent to press agencies or to publishers of newspapers or magazines;
   q. [lapsed]
   r. packaging means and other goods, whether ready for use or in loose parts, manufactured and fitted out for the transport of goods, tarpaulins and stowage material
      included, as well as objects for closing and labelling these packaging means, intended to be affixed to them, even if these objects are imported separately, all insofar as
      they:
      a. after having been exported from free circulation in order to export goods with them, are re-imported without having undergone any processing or working abroad;
      b. are imported to be used in the export of goods;
      c. are imported to be used for packaging goods extracted, manufactured, worked or processed in the domestic territory;
   s. raw materials, semi-finished products and machines, insofar as intended to be used in an enterprise in the domestic territory in order to carry on an industry;
   t. petroleum products, chemicals, catalysts and other auxiliary substances, insofar as intended to be processed in a petroleum refinery or to be used in the processing of
      petroleum and petroleum products;
   u. safety equipment designated by ministerial regulation, intended to be used in the commercial practice of fishing.
2. By National Decree containing general measures it may be determined that no import duties are levied on consumer goods for official receptions to be held by the Governor.
3. The commercial practice of a fishing business as referred to in paragraph 1(a) and (b) also includes fishing activities aimed at providing to a significant extent for the
   fisherman's livelihood.
4. The exemption referred to in paragraph 1(b) is granted only if:
   a. the permit holder uses the motor vehicle wholly or almost wholly for transport in the context of agriculture, livestock farming, horticulture or fishing;
   b. where a motor vehicle for the transport of goods has already been imported earlier with application of this exemption:
      1°. that motor vehicle has been in use for at least four years; or
      2°. the exemption was waived or destruction under official supervision took place in accordance with Article 32.
5. The exemption referred to in paragraph 1(c) is granted only if:
   a. the motor vehicle for the transport of persons is used wholly or almost wholly for public transport or taxi transport;
   b. where a motor vehicle for the transport of persons has already been imported earlier with application of this exemption:
      1°. that motor vehicle has been in use for at least three years; or
      2°. the exemption was waived or destruction under official supervision took place in accordance with Article 32.
6. The exemption referred to in paragraph 1(n) is granted only to hospitals designated by ministerial regulation.
7. The exemption referred to in paragraph 1(s) is granted only if the administration and administrative organisation of the enterprise are arranged such that the flow of goods
   can be checked in a simple manner.
8. Further rules for the implementation of the exemptions referred to in paragraph 1(s) and (t) may be set by ministerial regulation.
9. Under conditions to be set by the customs authorities, when an exemption granted in accordance with paragraph 4 or 5 is waived because of unforeseen circumstances, the
   import duties owed are determined according to the tariff of import duties and the customs value at the time the exemption is waived.
10. "Regular connections" or "regular transport" as referred to in paragraph 1(c) means: professional or commercial transport of persons or goods, accessible to everyone,
    according to a fixed timetable and over a fixed route.

##### § 20 Auxiliary material for the stowage and protection of goods during transport

###### Article 76

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of materials of various kinds, such as cables, straw, cloth, paper, cardboard, wood and plastic, that are used for the
stowage and protection, including thermal protection, of goods during transport to the domestic territory and that would not normally qualify for re-use.

###### Article 77

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally.

##### § 21 Fuels and lubricants in motor vehicles and containers

###### Article 78

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for:
   a. the fuels contained in the normal tanks of:
      1°. motor vehicles; or
      2°. containers fitted with devices specially adapted for refrigeration systems, oxygen supply systems, thermal insulation systems or other systems;
   b. the fuels contained in portable tanks in motor vehicles, up to a maximum of ten litres per vehicle;
   c. the lubricants contained in motor vehicles that correspond to the normal needs for their operation.
2. "Normal tanks" within the meaning of paragraph 1 means:
   a. the tanks permanently fitted by the manufacturer in or on all motor vehicles of the same type as the vehicle concerned, the arrangement of which allows the direct
      consumption of fuel, both for the propulsion of the vehicles and, where applicable, for the operation, during transport, of refrigeration and other systems;
   b. the tanks permanently fitted by the manufacturer in or on all containers of the same type as the container concerned, the arrangement of which allows the direct
      consumption of fuel for the operation, during transport, of refrigeration and other systems with which the containers are equipped for special purposes.

###### Article 79

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally.

###### Article 80

Fuels and lubricants imported with exemption may not be removed from the vehicle or container in which they were imported, except for storage during repairs to the vehicle or
container.

##### § 22 Coffins, urns and grave ornaments

###### Article 81

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:

a. coffins containing the mortal remains and urns containing the ashes of deceased persons, as well as flowers, wreaths and other ornaments that usually accompany them;
b. flowers, wreaths and other ornaments brought by persons living abroad who are travelling to a funeral or coming to decorate graves in the domestic territory, insofar as the
   nature and quantity of these goods show no commercial considerations;
c. empty coffins or urns imported to serve for the transport of bodies or ashes of deceased persons abroad.

###### Article 82

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally.

##### § 23 Outward processing

###### Article 83

1. Total or partial exemption from import duties is granted for goods that are temporarily exported abroad and are re-imported after having undergone a processing operation
   there.
2. In this section:
   a. **processing operation** means:
      1°. working goods, including assembling, mounting and adapting them to other goods;
      2°. processing goods;
      3°. repairing goods, including overhauling and adjusting them;
   b. **processed product** means a product resulting from one or more processing operations.

###### Article 84

1. The permit includes in any event:
   a. the period within which the processed products must be re-imported into the domestic territory;
   b. the calculation method for the amount to be exempted; and
   c. the manner in which the temporarily exported goods are identified in the processed products made from them that are to be re-imported.
2. The permit is granted only:
   a. to persons established in the domestic territory;
   b. if the identity of the temporarily exported goods can be established in the processed products; and
   c. if no enterprise in the domestic territory is able to meet the specific requirements of the processing on comparable conditions such as price, quality and delivery time.
3. The person concerned gives all cooperation to make it plausible that paragraph 2(c) is met.

###### Article 85

Only the processed products declared for import by the permit holder or for his account qualify for the exemption.

###### Article 86

1. The exemption is granted up to the amount of import duties that would be calculated on the import of the unprocessed goods if they were imported, at the time of import of
   the processed goods, from the country where those processed goods underwent the last processing operation.
2. In determining the amount, the import of the unprocessed goods is assumed in the condition in which they were at the time of the preceding export, taking into account the
   quantity and kind at the time of that export.

###### Article 87

1. Full exemption is granted for goods repaired free of charge, either under a contractual or statutory warranty obligation or because of a manufacturing defect, provided these
   defects were not taken into account when determining the customs value or for the application of the tariff of import duties at the time of the first import of the goods.
2. Article 84(2)(c) does not apply to the goods referred to in paragraph 1.
3. For goods repaired against payment, the amount equal to the repair costs is taken as the customs value, provided that payment of these costs is the only consideration of the
   permit holder.

###### Article 88

1. The declaration for export states:
   a. the nature of the treatment the goods will undergo;
   b. the value of the exported goods if they were imported at the time the declaration is made.
2. With the declaration for import, the declarant's copy of the declaration for export is submitted.

##### § 24 Returning goods

###### Article 89

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for goods which, after having been exported temporarily or for a certain purpose from free circulation in the domestic territory,
   are re-imported and:
   a. have undergone no treatment abroad other than that necessary to keep them in good condition;
   b. have undergone abroad treatments other than those needed to keep them in good condition, and after export from the domestic territory it has turned out that the goods:
      1°. showed defects and the treatments had the sole purpose of remedying those defects;
      2°. after use had begun, were unsuitable for the intended use and the treatments had the sole purpose of remedying this unsuitability.
2. The re-import of the goods must take place within a period of three months, counted from the time the goods were exported from the domestic territory.
3. If special circumstances or the nature of the purpose for which they were exported so require, the customs authorities may extend the period referred to in paragraph 2 for
   as long as necessary.

###### Article 90

1. The exemption referred to in Article 89(a) is granted only if, with the declaration for import, a proof of origin from the domestic territory is submitted, issued at the
   request of the person concerned, after the identity of the goods has been established, for the export.
2. If, owing to special circumstances, it could not be foreseen at the time of export that the goods would be re-imported, the customs authorities may grant exemption.

###### Article 91

If the exemption is granted according to the procedure referred to in Article 90(1), no permit is required.

###### Article 92

1. If the treatments referred to in Article 89(1)(b) would have given rise to the levying of import duties had the goods been exported under the exemption for outward
   processing, the provisions on that exemption apply.
2. The application referred to in paragraph 1 does not take place if the goods have undergone a repair or overhaul that:
   a. was necessary as a result of an unforeseen event abroad;
   b. did not raise the value of the returning goods above their value at the time of their export from the domestic territory;
   c. had necessarily to be carried out abroad.
3. The person concerned must demonstrate that the criterion in paragraph 2(c) is met.

##### § 25 Provisions, ship's stores, fuels and lubricants in ships and aircraft

###### Article 93

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. provisions and ship's stores on board ships entering;
   b. provisions present in aircraft of scheduled services in international traffic; and
   c. fuels and lubricants present in ships and aircraft entering and intended for their propulsion or lubrication, provided the goods are not removed from the ships or
      aircraft.
2. The exemption is granted only for the quantities reasonably necessary for consumption or use on board during the stay in the domestic territory.

###### Article 94

1. Provisions and ship's stores on board ships entering, as well as fuels and lubricants in those ships intended for their propulsion or lubrication, can be imported with
   exemption only if those goods are declared for import in writing.
2. No permit is required for import with this exemption.

##### § 26 Diplomatic exemptions

###### Article 95

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods intended for the personal use, use by resident family members included, of consular officials performing
   their functions in Sint Maarten, with the exception of honorary consuls, attached to consular posts established in the domestic territory, insofar as they do not have Dutch
   nationality, do not reside permanently in the domestic territory and otherwise carry out no professional or commercial activity on their own account in the domestic territory.
2. The exemption is granted only insofar as the state whose consular post is established in the domestic territory grants a corresponding exemption to the Kingdom of the
   Netherlands.
3. No security need be provided for the issue of the exemption document.
4. Consular officials who do not have Dutch nationality enjoy the exemption only in respect of goods imported at the time they first settle.

###### Article 96

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods intended for the official use, building and repair included, of consular posts established in the domestic
   territory that are headed by career consular officials.
2. The exemption is granted only if the head of the consular post confirms in a statement that the goods are intended for the official use of the consular post.
3. Article 95(2) and (3) apply correspondingly.

###### Article 97

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of chancery supplies that:
   a. are supplied by or at the request of the sending state to a consular post established in the domestic territory that is headed by an honorary official; and
   b. are intended for the official use of that post.
2. "Chancery supplies" means official emblems and documents as well as office furniture and office supplies.
3. The exemption is granted only if the head of the consular post confirms in a statement that the goods are intended for the official use of the consular post.
4. Article 95(2) and (3) apply correspondingly.

###### Article 98

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods brought in under tax-exemption provisions contained in treaties concluded with international organisations.

### Section 2 — Exemptions on temporary import

#### § 1 Inward processing

###### Article 99

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods that undergo a processing operation in the domestic territory.
2. Article 83(2) applies correspondingly.
3. The exemption takes the form of a refund or the application of a system of security, at the discretion of the customs authorities.
4. The import duties paid on the declaration for import of the goods are refunded, or the security provided is released, if these goods are re-exported in the form of
   processed products.
5. The declaration for import must state that use is being made of this exemption and must refer to the permit.
6. At the request of the customs authorities the permit must be attached to the declaration for import.

###### Article 100

1. The permit includes in any event:
   a. the period within which the processed products must be re-exported from the domestic territory;
   b. the calculation method for the amount to be exempted; and
   c. the manner in which the temporarily imported goods are identified in the processed products made from them that are to be re-exported.
2. The permit is granted only:
   a. to persons established in the domestic territory;
   b. if the goods to be imported can be recognised in the processed products; and
   c. if no enterprise in the domestic territory is able to produce the raw materials or semi-finished products needed for the processing on comparable conditions such as price,
      quality and delivery time.
3. The person concerned gives all cooperation to make it plausible that paragraph 2(c) is met.

###### Article 101

1. The permit holder submits a request for refund of import duties, or for release of the security, to the customs authorities within three months after the day on which the
   processed products obtained from the imported goods:
   a. were exported from the customs territory under the supervision of the customs authorities;
   b. were stored in a customs warehouse with a view to later export.
2. When special circumstances justify it, the customs authorities may deviate from the period referred to in paragraph 1.

#### § 2 Means of transport imported temporarily

###### Article 102

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of means of transport, and the normal equipment of the means of transport imported together with them, belonging to:
   a. persons who have their normal place of residence abroad and which are imported by the owners of those means of transport or by other persons who have their normal place
      of residence abroad, and are used for their personal use on the occasion of a temporary visit;
   b. enterprises that have their registered office abroad and use the means of transport for the commercial transport of persons or goods, whether or not against payment,
      reward or other material benefit, provided this transport begins and ends abroad.
2. For the application of paragraph 1, *personal use* means use for purposes other than the transport of persons against payment, reward or other material benefit, the
   commercial transport of goods, or other professional use.

###### Article 103

No permit is required for import with this exemption.

###### Article 104

1. The exemption referred to in Article 102(1)(a) is granted for the import of passenger vehicles if a written declaration for import has been made by surrendering:
   a. a *carnet de passages en douane* issued by and under the liability of an association affiliated to the Alliance Internationale de Tourisme or the Fédération
      Internationale de l'Automobile; or
   b. an identity card, issued by the competent authorities.
2. Passenger vehicles declared for import in accordance with paragraph 1(a) must be re-exported within the validity of the carnet, but at the latest 12 months after the date of
   import.

3. Passenger vehicles declared for import in accordance with paragraph 1(b) must be re-exported within 30 days after the date of import.
4. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 3 on grounds of special circumstances.
5. The document issued on the declaration for import is the carnet or identity card that was surrendered.
6. No security need be provided for the issue of the carnet.

###### Article 105

1. The exemption for enterprises referred to in Article 102(1)(b) is granted for the import of means of transport used for the transport of persons or for the commercial
   transport of goods, for semi-trailers, for trailers and for container chassis.
2. The exemption applies only to enterprises that:
   a. are established in a state that applies a similar exemption; and
   b. hold a permit for road transport issued by the competent authority.
3. The declaration is made by surrendering:
   a. a triptyque or a *carnet de passages en douane* issued for each means of transport separately by and under the liability of an association affiliated to the Alliance
      Internationale de Tourisme or the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile; or
   b. a declaration for the temporary import of motor vehicles.

###### Article 106

1. The exemption is granted for the import of pleasure craft if a written declaration for import has been made by surrendering:
   a. a *carnet de passages en douane*, issued by and under the liability of an association affiliated to the Alliance Internationale de Tourisme; or
   b. a statement issued by the customs authorities concerning the temporary import with exemption of pleasure craft.
2. The declaration for import must be made within 24 hours after entering the domestic territory.
3. Pleasure craft declared for import in accordance with paragraph 1(a) must be re-exported within the validity of the carnet, but at the latest within 12 months after the date
   of import.
4. Pleasure craft declared for import in accordance with paragraph 1(b) must be re-exported within 30 days after the date of import.
5. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 4 on grounds of special circumstances.
6. The document issued on the declaration for import is the carnet or statement that was surrendered.
7. No security need be provided for the issue of the carnet.

###### Article 107

It is prohibited to use a means of transport imported with exemption without being able to show the document issued by the customs authorities as proof of this exemption, the
validity of which has not expired.

#### § 3 Containers imported temporarily

###### Article 108

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. containers imported filled to be re-exported empty or filled, or imported empty to be re-exported filled;
   b. the normal accessories and normal equipment of the containers, provided these are imported with the containers; and
   c. loose parts imported for the repair of a specific container that has already been imported temporarily with exemption.
2. "Containers" means storage means that:
   a. have an internal volume of at least one cubic metre;
   b. are of a durable character and for that reason sufficiently strong for repeated use;
   c. are specially designed to facilitate the transport of goods without intermediate loading and unloading of the goods themselves;
   d. are fitted with devices that facilitate handling of the container, in particular when transferring from one means of transport to or onto another; and
   e. are designed so that they can be easily filled and emptied.

###### Article 109

1. Containers imported with exemption must be re-exported within three months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances.

###### Article 110

No permit is required for import with this exemption.

#### § 4 Pallets imported temporarily

###### Article 111

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of pallets that are imported empty or in use in order to export or import goods with them, and are to be re-exported after
having remained in the domestic territory for at most 12 months.

###### Article 112

1. No permit is required for import with exemption of pallets imported filled.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally.

#### § 5 Professional equipment imported temporarily

###### Article 113

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. professional equipment needed by a representative of the press, radio or television who comes to the domestic territory to make reports or to make broadcasts or
      recordings for programmes;
   b. film equipment needed by a person who comes to the domestic territory to make one or more films;
   c. other professional equipment needed for the exercise of the trade or profession of a person who comes to the domestic territory to carry out a specific job, with the
      exception of equipment intended to be used for domestic transport or for the manufacture or packaging of goods;
   d. the auxiliary equipment belonging to the equipment referred to in (a) to (c);
   e. parts imported later with a view to repairing equipment as referred to in (a) to (c).
2. The exemption is granted only if the professional equipment:
   a. belongs to a person established abroad;
   b. is imported by a person established abroad; and
   c. is used solely by, or under the direction of, the person who goes to the domestic territory.
3. The condition in paragraph 2(c) does not apply to film equipment imported for making films in performance of a co-production agreement concluded with a person established
   in the domestic territory.

###### Article 114

1. The equipment imported with exemption is re-exported within six months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances.

#### § 6 Exhibition goods imported temporarily

###### Article 115

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of goods intended to be shown or used at exhibitions, insofar as they can be counted among the following categories:
   a. goods intended to be exhibited or with which demonstrations will be given at exhibitions;
   b. goods intended to be used for the presentation of imported products at an exhibition;
   c. material, including interpretation installations, sound-recording equipment and films of an educational, scientific or cultural nature, intended to be used at
      international meetings, conferences and congresses;
   d. products obtained during the exhibitions from temporarily imported goods, machines, apparatus or animals.
2. Article 70(2) applies correspondingly.
3. The exemption is not granted if the goods are intended for private exhibitions organised in shops or business premises with a view to the sale of foreign goods.
4. The goods for which temporary import is allowed may not be lent or hired out, nor used for payment, and may not be taken outside the place where exhibitions and the like
   are held.

###### Article 116

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within one month after the end of the exhibition, it being understood that re-export must take place at the latest 12
   months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances.

###### Article 117

Permission as referred to in Article 32 may be granted without there having to be special circumstances that could not be foreseen at the import.

#### § 7 Educational material imported temporarily

###### Article 118

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of educational material, parts and accessories for this material, and tools specially designed for the
   maintenance, checking, adjustment or repair of that material, insofar as these goods:
   a. are imported by recognised institutions and used under the supervision and responsibility of those institutions;
   b. are used on a non-profit basis;
   c. in view of their purpose, are imported in reasonable quantities; and
   d. remain the property of a person established abroad.
2. For the application of paragraph 1:
   a. **educational material** means all material used for education or vocational training and in particular models, instruments, apparatus, machines and accessories;
   b. **recognised institutions** means, as designated by ministerial regulation:
      1°. public institutions or public-utility institutions whose main activity is education or scientific research;
      2°. private institutions whose main activity is education or scientific research.

###### Article 119

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within a period set by the customs authorities. The period depends on the use of the goods, but lasts no longer than **six
   months** after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most three months.

#### § 8 Scientific material imported temporarily

###### Article 120

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. scientific material, parts and accessories for this material, and tools specially designed for the maintenance, checking, adjustment or repair of that material; and
   b. parts of scientific material imported later with a view to repairing scientific material previously imported with exemption,
   insofar as these goods:
   1°. are imported by recognised institutions and used under the supervision and responsibility of those institutions;
   2°. are used on a non-profit basis;
   3°. in view of their purpose, are imported in reasonable quantities; and
   4°. remain the property of a person established abroad.
2. For the application of paragraph 1:
   a. **scientific material** means the instruments, apparatus, machines and accessories used for scientific research and education;
   b. **recognised institutions** means, as designated by ministerial regulation:
      1°. public institutions or public-utility institutions whose main activity is education or scientific research;
      2°. private institutions whose main activity is education or scientific research.
3. Article 114 applies correspondingly.

#### § 9 Medical-surgical and laboratory material imported temporarily

###### Article 121

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of medical-surgical and laboratory material on condition that:
   a. it concerns an incidental consignment;
   b. the material has been made available on loan; and
   c. the material will be used for diagnostic or therapeutic purposes by hospitals designated by ministerial regulation.
2. "Incidental consignment" as referred to in paragraph 1(a) means any consignment of medical-surgical and laboratory material at the request of hospitals that, owing to
   special circumstances, urgently need this material to supplement their insufficient own equipment.
3. Article 119 applies correspondingly.

#### § 10 Disaster-relief material imported temporarily

###### Article 122

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of material intended to be used in combating the effects of disasters, on condition that the material:
   a. has been made available free of charge; and
   b. is intended for government organisations and institutions to be designated by ministerial regulation.
2. Only institutions that do not seek profit and that work mainly in the field of relief or charity qualify for designation by ministerial regulation.
3. No security need be provided for the issue of the exemption document.
4. The customs authorities may require an inventory of the goods, together with a written undertaking regarding re-export.

###### Article 123

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within a period set by the customs authorities. The period depends on the use of the goods, but lasts no longer than **24
   months** after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most 12 months.

#### § 11 Packaging imported temporarily

###### Article 124

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of packaging on condition that it:
   a. when imported filled, is intended to be re-exported empty or filled;
   b. when imported empty, is intended to be re-exported filled.
2. For the application of paragraph 1, "packaging" means:
   a. containers used or intended to be used for the external or internal packing of goods;
   b. material on which goods are rolled, bound or fixed, or which is intended for that.
3. The exemption is not granted for packing materials such as straw, paper, fibreglass and wood shavings imported in bulk.

###### Article 125

The declaration for import for packaging imported filled may be made orally.

###### Article 126

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within a period set by the customs authorities. The length of the period depends on the use of the goods, but lasts no
   longer than:
   a. six months after the date of import, if the packaging is imported filled;
   b. three months after the date of import, if the packaging is imported empty.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the periods referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most three and two months respectively.

###### Article 127

Packaging imported with exemption may not be used again in the domestic territory, except with a view to exporting goods from the domestic territory. If the packaging was
imported filled, this prohibition applies only from the time it has been emptied of its contents.

#### § 12 Casting moulds, measuring instruments, samples and other goods imported temporarily

###### Article 128

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:

a. casting moulds, dies, printing blocks (clichés), drawings, designs and other similar objects intended for a person established in the domestic territory;
b. measuring, checking and verification instruments and other similar objects intended for a person established in the domestic territory to be used during a manufacturing
   process;
c. goods of whatever kind that must be subjected to tests, experiments or demonstrations, with the exception of tests, experiments or demonstrations that constitute a
   profit-making activity;
d. samples representative of a certain group of products and intended to be shown or demonstrated with a view to placing orders for similar goods;
e. special tools and instruments made available free of charge to a person established in the domestic territory and intended to be used in the manufacture of goods that are
   to be exported in their entirety, on condition that such special tools remain the property of a person established abroad.

###### Article 129

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within a period set by the customs authorities. The length of the period depends on the use of the goods, but lasts no
   longer than 12 months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most six months.

#### § 13 Replacement machines imported temporarily

###### Article 130

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of replacement means of production that are temporarily made available free of charge to the importer by or on the initiative of the supplier of:

a. similar means of production that are imported later to be released for free circulation; or
b. means of production that are put back into use after having been repaired.

###### Article 131

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within six months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most three months.

#### § 14 Films, information carriers and sound carriers imported temporarily

###### Article 132

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. cinematographic films, exposed and developed, positive, intended to be viewed before they are put into commercial circulation;
   b. films that show the nature of products or the working of foreign equipment, on condition that they are not intended for public showing against payment;
   c. information carriers and sound carriers, with recordings, for non-commercial purposes, made available free of charge to a person established in the domestic territory.
2. Article 131 applies correspondingly.

#### § 15 Personal baggage of travellers imported temporarily

###### Article 133

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of personal goods that a traveller carries himself or in his personal baggage for the duration of his stay in the
   domestic territory.
2. For the application of this exemption, *personal goods* means all garments and other new or used objects intended for the personal use of the traveller.

###### Article 134

1. Unless the customs authorities decide otherwise, no permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. The declaration for import may be made orally. The customs authorities may demand that security be provided for goods in respect of which abuse is feared because of their
   nature, quantity or value.

###### Article 135

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within three months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances, up to at most the end of the period for which
   the competent authorities have permitted the traveller to stay in the domestic territory.

#### § 16 Tourist publicity material imported temporarily

###### Article 136

Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of tourist publicity material designated by ministerial regulation that belongs to a person established abroad and is
imported in reasonable quantities in the light of its intended use.

###### Article 137

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within 12 months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most six months.

#### § 17 Welfare goods for seafarers imported temporarily

###### Article 138

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of welfare goods intended for seafarers on board ships in international traffic and which:
   a. are used on board foreign ships or are brought ashore to be used temporarily on land by the crew for a period no longer than the berthing period in the port; or
   b. are imported to be used temporarily in cultural or social institutions.
2. For the application of paragraph 1:
   a. **welfare goods** means material designated by ministerial regulation that is intended for the activities of seafarers in the cultural, educational, recreational,
      religious or sporting field;
   b. **seafarers** means all persons carried on board a ship who are charged with tasks related to the operation or service of that ship at sea;
   c. **cultural or social institutions** means meeting centres, clubs and recreation rooms for seafarers, run by official bodies or by religious or other non-profit
      institutions, as well as places of worship where services for seafarers are regularly held.
3. The permit must be applied for by the master of the ship or by the head of the cultural or social institution.

###### Article 139

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within six months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most three months.

#### § 18 Goods imported temporarily in special circumstances and without economic consequences

###### Article 140

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. objects of artists and fairground attractions;
   b. sports equipment and gear intended for participants in sports competitions to be held in the domestic territory;
   c. animals for purposes designated by ministerial regulation.
2. The exemption applies only if the goods belong to persons who have their normal place of residence abroad.

###### Article 141

1. The goods imported with exemption are re-exported within three months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may allow an extension of the period referred to in paragraph 1 on grounds of special circumstances by at most one month.

#### § 19 Provisions, ship's stores, fuels and lubricants imported temporarily

###### Article 142

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. provisions and ship's stores on board ships, not being houseboats;
   b. provisions present in aircraft of scheduled services in international traffic;
   c. fuels and lubricants present in entering means of transport and intended for their propulsion or lubrication.
2. Exemption is granted only if the storage spaces intended for the goods imported with exemption can be sealed.

###### Article 143

1. No permit is required for import with this exemption.
2. Provisions and ship's stores on board entering ships, not being houseboats, as well as fuels and lubricants in those ships and intended for their propulsion or lubrication,
   can be imported with exemption only if those goods are declared for import in writing.
3. In cases other than those referred to in paragraph 2, the goods are declared for import in writing only if the customs authorities consider this desirable in view of the
   nature, quantity or value of the goods.

###### Article 144

The goods imported with exemption are re-exported together with the means of transport with which the goods were brought in.

#### § 20 Temporary import of heavy machines and rolling, floating and other equipment

###### Article 145

1. Exemption from import duties is granted for the import of:
   a. heavy machines and rolling, floating and other equipment, imported to serve solely for use in carrying out:
      1°. works abroad;
      2°. dredging works, hydraulic engineering works and large road and other construction works in the domestic territory designated by ministerial regulation;
   b. the normal accessories and normal equipment of the machines and equipment, provided these are imported together;
   c. loose parts imported for the repair of a specific machine or specific equipment that has already been imported temporarily with exemption.
2. For imports referred to in paragraph 1(a)(2°), import duties are levied, in a manner to be determined further by ministerial regulation, on the difference between the value of
   the goods at import and their value at the end of use.

###### Article 146

1. The heavy machines imported with exemption are re-exported within one month after the end of the work required, it being understood that re-export must take place at the
   latest 12 months after the date of import.
2. The customs authorities may, on a reasoned request, deviate from the period of 12 months referred to in paragraph 1.

## Chapter IV — Refund of import duties

### Section 1 — General provisions

#### Article 147

For the application of this chapter, *refund* (teruggaaf) means:

a. the full or partial return of import duties already paid; or
b. if the import duties have not yet been paid, the full or partial non-levying of the import duties charged.

#### Article 148

1. Refund takes place, subject to Article 152(3), on a written application to be submitted to the customs authorities.
2. The application for refund is submitted within **six months** after the declaration for import was made.
3. The application is submitted by the person obliged to pay the import duties or who has paid them, or by the person who has succeeded to his rights and obligations, or by the
   representative of the persons mentioned.
4. The request for refund must be accompanied by all evidence available to the applicant. When the customs authorities consider it necessary, they may set a period within which
   the applicant must submit additional evidence.
5. The customs authorities decide within **nine months** of receipt of the application. The decision is reasoned.
6. Refund is granted to the person obliged to pay the import duties or who has paid them, or to the person who has succeeded to his rights and obligations.

#### Article 149

Refund is not granted if the amount of the import duties to be refunded, per declaration for import or document, is less than **NAf 15**.

#### Article 150

1. If, when applying Article 154 or Article 156, the goods must be re-exported, this re-export does not take place before a decision has been taken on the application for
   refund.
2. In cases giving cause, the customs authorities may allow re-export to take place before a decision on the application has been taken.

#### Article 151

It is prohibited to provide incorrect or incomplete data or to perform acts that lead or could lead to the wrongful refund of import duties.

### Section 2 — Cases of refund

#### § 1 No liability; amount of import duties higher than owed

##### Article 152

1. Refund of import duties is granted insofar as the amount determined of these duties:
   a. relates to goods for which no liability arose or for which the liability ceased to exist otherwise than by payment of the amount; or
   b. is higher than the amount that could lawfully be collected.
2. The applicant must demonstrate that the criterion in paragraph 1 is met.
3. The customs authorities proceed to refund of their own motion when, during the period referred to in Article 148(2), they themselves establish the existence of a circumstance
   as referred to in paragraph 1.

#### § 2 Goods declared by mistake

##### Article 153

1. Refund of import duties is granted if:
   a. the amount of the duties determined relates to goods that, as a result of a mistake, were released for free circulation instead of being placed under another customs
      procedure;
   b. the goods, after having been declared for import by mistake, have not been used under conditions other than those laid down in the customs procedure for which they were
      intended;
   c. the goods to which the request relates are the goods that were declared for import;
   d. the goods were already, before they were declared for import, intended to be placed under another customs procedure and at that moment already met the conditions of
      that customs procedure, and the goods are without delay declared for the customs procedure for which they are intended.
2. The applicant must demonstrate that the criteria in paragraph 1(a) and (b) are met.

#### § 3 Refused goods

##### Article 154

1. Refund of import duties is granted if:
   a. the amount of the duties determined relates to:
      1°. goods refused by the importer because they show defects or because for some reason they do not comply with the provisions of the contract on the basis of which these
          goods were imported; or
      2°. goods that were damaged during their transport to the office where they were declared for free circulation, or during their storage in a customs warehouse;
   b. the goods showed defects, or did not comply with the provisions of the contract on the basis of which the import took place, already before they were placed at the
      declarant's disposal or before permission was granted to follow their destination;
   c. the goods have not been used, which does not include a beginning of use needed to establish that the goods show defects or do not meet the provisions of the contract on
      the basis of which the import took place;
   d. the goods to which the request for refund relates are the goods that were imported; and
   e. the goods are destroyed under the supervision of the customs authorities, or are re-exported.
2. The applicant must demonstrate that the criteria in paragraph 1(a) to (d) are met.

##### Article 155

Refund of import duties for the goods referred to in Article 154(1) is not granted in cases where:

a. the defects were taken into account in drawing up the conditions of the contract on the basis of which the goods were declared for import, in particular in fixing the price;
   or
b. the importer sold the goods after it was established that they show these defects or do not comply with the provisions of the contract.

#### § 4 Goods in special situations

##### Article 156

Refund of import duties is granted if:

a. the amount of these duties relates to goods:
   1°. that were sent to the addressee by mistake of the sender;
   2°. that prove unsuitable for the use intended by the addressee because of an evident error in the order;
   3°. whose use for the purposes intended by the addressee is impossible or considerably restricted as a result of measures of a general nature taken by the competent
       authority after the date on which the goods were released for free circulation; or
   4°. that were delivered to the addressee after the binding delivery dates of the contract;
b. the goods have not been sold or used, which does not include a beginning of use needed to assess the goods;
c. the goods to which the request for refund relates are the goods that were imported; and
d. the goods are destroyed under the supervision of the customs authorities, or are re-exported.

##### Article 157

Refund of import duties is granted for goods purchased in the domestic territory under the refund provisions contained in treaties concerning international organisations to
which Sint Maarten is a party.
